## Controlling for department size

**The last column is the one to read.** It is how many of the ten departments the fitted score ranked highest are also among the ten that simply buy the most. Where that number is close to ten, the model has rediscovered department size and the evidence signals have added nothing a list of the biggest buyers would not give you for free.

## The finding

Uncontrolled, the strongest evidence signal is `audit_it` at **1.46x**. Inside a size stratum, 0 of 15 feature-by-stratum comparisons show a lift above 1.0 whose confidence interval excludes the stratum's own base rate.

**That is a null result on the interesting claim.** Every evidence signal — Auditor General findings, stated departmental intent, incumbent contracts running down — collapses to roughly no effect once you compare departments of similar size. The raw lifts in the marginals table are real arithmetic and mean almost nothing: the Auditor General audits large departments, large departments tender constantly, and `audit_it` was reading the second fact through the first.

The combined score does beat chance at a ten-department alert budget. It should: ranking federal organizations by how much IT they buy is genuinely predictive of whether they will buy IT again, and the overlap column shows that is substantially what the fit learned. That is worth knowing and it is not the pre-RFP thesis. The claim under test was that convergent public evidence sees a procurement coming that a list of the biggest buyers would miss, and on three fiscal years of federal data it does not.

What this does NOT establish: that the evidence is worthless for the question a human actually asks. This tests *whether a department tenders anything relevant at all in a year* — a coarse target with a 68.6% base rate and, in the largest third of departments, 94%. There is almost no room to be right in a way that counts. The dossier's real use has always been *what* is coming and *why now*, at the level of a specific system with a named incumbent, and nothing here speaks to that. It is a different experiment and it needs a finer target than this panel can carry.

## What this does and does not establish

- **n is 156 department-years across 3 complete fiscal year(s), 107 of them positive.** The interval on the pooled base rate alone is 60.9-75.4%. Every per-signal figure rests on a fraction of that n and its interval is correspondingly wider - several are wide enough to cover both a real effect and none at all.
- **The base rate is 68.6%.** A rule that alerts on every department is right that often. Nothing here is worth anything unless it beats that number, and beating it by a few points inside a wide interval is not evidence that it does.
- **`prior_year_hit` is the baseline that matters.** Procurement is autocorrelated. An evidence signal that does not beat "they tendered last year" is telling you about the calendar.
- **Departmental-plan features are weakest where they would matter most.** Sixteen organizations file no planning prose at all, and they include DND, GAC, the RCMP, CBSA and SSC - the largest federal IT buyers. A zero on `plan_intent` is usually silence, not an absence of intent.
